# Limits of br-sandbox-chart 0.4.0

Status words: tested offline (the test is named, all in `test-sandbox-chart-operator-docs.sh` unless another file is named), read from a page or a file (named), inferred (a reading; no file, no page and no test shows it), not tested on a cluster. A limit stated here is not a promise that the chart closes it.

## Two sets of fence objects

Two sets of objects have two names in this chart.

**The fence-check set** is what the fence check of the keeper reads in a managed namespace: the labels of the namespace, the ResourceQuota `br-sandbox-quota`, the NetworkPolicy `br-sandbox-default-deny` (its shape: empty pod selector, both directions, no allow rule), and the binding `br-sandbox-pod` that selects the namespace. The check also reads that no network policy outside the closed list `br-sandbox-default-deny`, `br-sandbox-egress-allow` and `br-sandbox-egress-full` is in the namespace. It reads no Role and no RoleBinding, and it does not read the template ConfigMap: a missing template is `TemplateAbsent` at the pool step, not a fence fault. This is read from the README of keeper 0.2.0 (section Fence); it is not tested in this repository. That the fence part renders no network policy outside the closed list, in every mode, is tested offline (`test-sandbox-chart-network.sh`).

**The fence-object set** is what the policy `br-sandbox-fence` protects from a project identity in a managed namespace: the `networkpolicies`, `resourcequotas`, `limitranges`, `roles` and `rolebindings` of any name, the ServiceAccount named `sandbox`, and the ConfigMaps whose name starts with `br-sandbox-` (the adapter document, the template, and the other ConfigMaps of the chart in the namespace). It holds more than the fence-check set. The template ConfigMap `br-sandbox-pod-template` is in the fence-object set only. The kinds, the operations and the exemptions of the policy are tested offline with a model, not on a cluster (`test-sandbox-chart-fence-policy.sh`).

The check proves names and the shape of one policy. It does not prove the content of the other fence objects: a person who can write them in the namespace can make a fence that passes the check and protects nothing. The control of who can write them is the policy `br-sandbox-fence`, and its limit is stated below.

## The binding of the update

The fence check of the keeper reads the binding `br-sandbox-pod`. It does not read `br-sandbox-pod-update`. If that binding is absent, an identity with the patch right on pods can change the image of a pod after its admission, and the keeper does not see it.

Only an identity with a write right on admission policy bindings at cluster scope can remove a binding: that is the platform. The runner and the keeper have no such right: no Role and no ClusterRole of any render of the chart writes an admission policy binding (`check_no_role_of_any_render_writes_an_admission_binding` in `test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`, and `check_the_controller_holds_no_forbidden_right` in `test-sandbox-chart-operator-rights.sh` for the keeper). A project identity is not an object of the chart: that it holds no such right is a condition on the platform, not tested here. The two bindings are one template under one condition, so no value turns off one of them alone (`test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`). The bindings must not be removed while pools run: with keeper 0.2, if the binding `br-sandbox-pod` goes, every managed namespace is not fenced and every pool is removed until the binding is back (read from the README of keeper 0.2.0).

## Limits

- **One `client_id` for a scope.** All sandbox namespaces of one scope share one `client_id`, so the store prefix does not separate two pools of one product and environment (tested: two namespaces of the scope `sbx-alpha` render the same `client_id`). The separation between two products rests on a rule of the platform: a project can declare a namespace only under its own name prefix. The chart cannot tell two projects apart when they act under one GitOps identity; that rule is a fact of the platform repository, not read or tested here. One entry of `scopes` for each product and environment is needed, one time, before its projects can declare a pool: a name outside every scope is refused (`BR_SANDBOX_SCOPE`).
- **No `SandboxPool`, no pod.** A managed namespace with no `SandboxPool` admits no new pod, the probe pod of the network check included (policy model, `test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`; that the API server does the same is not tested on a cluster).
- **Two resources in one namespace.** A pod must pass the rules against every `SandboxPool` of its namespace, so a second resource with another image or a smaller size stops the new pods of the namespace until it is removed (policy model, `test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`).
- **A size above the maximum.** A size above `limits.size` is not refused at the resource: the definition does not know the platform maximum. Its pods are refused at pod time (policy model), and the LimitRange `max` of the namespace is `limits.size` (tested: the render of the LimitRange). The chart sets no cap on `maxReplicas`: the quota refuses the pods above `limits.pods` plus `quota.extra_pods` (tested: the render of the quota).
- **No rule on who creates pods.** No rule says who may create a pod in a managed namespace. A rule on the creator was considered and not made: the controller of a ReplicaSet creates the pods of every ReplicaSet, a ReplicaSet of a project too. Between the creation of a namespace and the arrival of its fence, an identity that can create pods in it is judged by the pod policy only. The pods of the warm pool are gated by other means: the account `sandbox` that rule 14 requires is a fence object that only a writer makes, and the keeper makes no ReplicaSet before the fence is there (a fact of keeper 0.2, not tested in this repository). The window is not closed and not tested on a cluster.
- **The stamper is off by default** (`stamper.enabled` is `false`) and is not proved on a cluster: its text is tested offline, and the project side is proved by the battery only when the fence part is applied by the platform. `STAMPER.md` says what is inferred and which conditions it puts on the platform.
- **The fence-object control in the fallback shape.** When the platform applies the fence part with one GitOps identity that the projects share, that identity must be a writer, and the policy `br-sandbox-fence` then does not stop a project from writing a fence object through it. The control holds only if the project and the platform act under different identities, or if the GitOps project of the project refuses it the fence kinds. The chart cannot see which case the cluster is in (`POLICIES.md`, section Fence objects).
- **A project can declare any image in its own pool.** The pod policy compares the image of the agent with the `image` of the resource, as written. It says "this pool declared it", not "this repository is trusted". `registry_mirror` does not change that image.
- **`imagePullPolicy: Always` on the agent.** The cost is one registry call at each pod start; a start can fail when the registry does not answer (not tested on a cluster).
- **`kubectl debug`** on a sandbox pod is refused for every identity (rule 15); debug a copy in another namespace.
- **The keeper namespace.** With `operator.enabled` the chart renders in the system namespace `br-sandbox-default-deny` and `br-sandbox-egress-keeper`: egress only to `network.api_server_addresses`, `network.store_cidrs` and the cluster DNS. It is not tested on a cluster, and the probes of the kubelet under a default-deny policy depend on the network plugin.
- **The definition.** It has one version, `v1alpha1`, and no conversion: a later change of its schema has no conversion between versions (inferred). With two releases of the chart in one cluster the chart cannot see that one of them sets `crds.install`.
- **The first seconds of a fresh pod.** The network policies bind a fresh pod only once the network plugin enforces them; the window is not closed (README, The enforcement window).
- **The proxied modes.** The modes `allow` and `full` with a credential set need inputs that a project would choose and that widen its egress: the allow list, the credential set, the proxy address and the proxy CA. In 0.4.0 they are values of the platform on the entry of `namespaces`. The stamper has no rule for them, and the namespace policy grants them only through `grants.modes`. Who gives the allow list and who approves it is not decided.
