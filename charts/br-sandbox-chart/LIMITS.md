# Limits of br-sandbox-chart 0.4.0

Status words: tested offline (the test is named, all in `test-sandbox-chart-operator-docs.sh` unless another file is named), read from a page or a file (named), inferred (a reading; no file, no page and no test shows it), not tested on a cluster. A limit stated here is not a promise that the chart closes it.

## Two sets of fence objects

Two sets of objects have two names in this chart.

**The fence-check set** is what the fence check of the keeper reads in a managed namespace: the labels of the namespace, the ResourceQuota `br-sandbox-quota`, the NetworkPolicy `br-sandbox-default-deny` (its shape: empty pod selector, both directions, no allow rule), and the binding `br-sandbox-pod` that selects the namespace. The check also reads that no network policy outside the closed list `br-sandbox-default-deny`, `br-sandbox-egress-allow` and `br-sandbox-egress-full` is in the namespace. It reads no Role and no RoleBinding, and it does not read the template ConfigMap: a missing template is `TemplateAbsent` at the pool step, not a fence fault. This is read from the README of keeper 0.2.0 (section Fence); it is not tested in this repository. That the fence part renders no network policy outside the closed list, in every mode, is tested offline (`test-sandbox-chart-network.sh`). The fence check of the keeper does not cover the `{namespace}-egress` namespace: an egress namespace has no managed label and the keeper does not judge it, so a NetworkPolicy added by hand there is not seen (reported by the operator of a test cluster; no record in this repository; not proved here: with the managed label the same policy makes the keeper delete the ReplicaSet and report the namespace as not fenced).

**The fence-object set** is what the policy `br-sandbox-fence` protects from a project identity in a managed namespace: the `networkpolicies`, `resourcequotas`, `limitranges`, `roles` and `rolebindings` of any name, the ServiceAccount named `sandbox`, and the ConfigMaps whose name starts with `br-sandbox-` (the adapter document, the template, and the other ConfigMaps of the chart in the namespace). It holds more than the fence-check set. The template ConfigMap `br-sandbox-pod-template` is in the fence-object set only. The kinds, the operations and the exemptions of the policy are tested offline with a model, not on a cluster (`test-sandbox-chart-fence-policy.sh`).

The check proves names and the shape of one policy. It does not prove the content of the other fence objects: a person who can write them in the namespace can make a fence that passes the check and protects nothing. The control of who can write them is the policy `br-sandbox-fence`, and its limit is stated below.

## The binding of the update

The fence check of the keeper reads the binding `br-sandbox-pod`. It does not read `br-sandbox-pod-update`. If that binding is absent, an identity with the patch right on pods can change the image of a pod after its admission, and the keeper does not see it.

Only an identity with a write right on admission policy bindings at cluster scope can remove a binding: that is the platform. The runner and the keeper have no such right: no Role and no ClusterRole of any render of the chart writes an admission policy binding (`check_no_role_of_any_render_writes_an_admission_binding` in `test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`, and `check_the_controller_holds_no_forbidden_right` in `test-sandbox-chart-operator-rights.sh` for the keeper). A project identity is not an object of the chart: that it holds no such right is a condition on the platform, not tested here. The two bindings are one template under one condition, so no value turns off one of them alone (`test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`). The bindings must not be removed while pools run: with keeper 0.2, if the binding `br-sandbox-pod` goes, every managed namespace is not fenced and every pool is removed until the binding is back (read from the README of keeper 0.2.0). If the binding `br-sandbox-pod` is restored with `kubectl apply`, the next `helm upgrade` of the operator release fails on a server-side-apply conflict and needs `--force-conflicts` (reported by the operator of a test cluster; no record in this repository; not proved here).

## Limits

- **One `client_id` for a scope.** All sandbox namespaces of one scope share one `client_id`, so the store prefix does not separate two pools of one product and environment (tested: two namespaces of the scope `sbx-alpha` render the same `client_id`). The separation between two products rests on a rule of the platform: a project can declare a namespace only under its own name prefix. The chart cannot tell two projects apart when they act under one GitOps identity; that rule is a fact of the platform repository, not read or tested here. One entry of `scopes` for each product and environment is needed, one time, before its projects can declare a pool: a name outside every scope is refused (`BR_SANDBOX_SCOPE`).
- **No `SandboxPool`, no pod.** A managed namespace with no `SandboxPool` admits no new pod, the probe pod of the network check included (policy model, `test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`; that the API server does the same is not tested on a cluster).
- **Two resources in one namespace.** A pod must pass the rules against every `SandboxPool` of its namespace, so a second resource with another image or a smaller size stops the new pods of the namespace until it is removed (policy model, `test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`).
- **A size above the maximum.** A size above `limits.size` is not refused at the resource: the definition does not know the platform maximum. Its pods are refused at pod time (policy model), and the LimitRange `max` of the namespace is `limits.size` (tested: the render of the LimitRange). The chart sets no cap on `maxReplicas`: the quota refuses the pods above `limits.pods` plus `quota.extra_pods` (tested: the render of the quota).
- **No rule on who creates pods.** No rule says who may create a pod in a managed namespace. A rule on the creator was considered and not made: the controller of a ReplicaSet creates the pods of every ReplicaSet, a ReplicaSet of a project too. Between the creation of a namespace and the arrival of its fence, an identity that can create pods in it is judged by the pod policy only. The pods of the warm pool are gated by other means: the account `sandbox` that `BR_SANDBOX_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` requires is a fence object that only a writer makes, and the keeper makes no ReplicaSet before the fence is there (a fact of keeper 0.2, not tested in this repository). The window is not closed and not tested on a cluster.
- **The stamper is off by default** (`stamper.enabled` is `false`) and no cluster run of it is recorded in this repository: its text is tested offline. The automatic stamping passed once on a test cluster with the policy engine (reported by the operator of that cluster; no record in this repository), for one namespace in mode none. Not proved: the platform cluster and the rights of the policy engine there, a second project at the same time, mode full, a namespace already fenced by the fence release, a policy removal while stamped namespaces exist. The project side is proved only once a cluster run of the install in `INSTALL.md` has passed; none is recorded at this version. `STAMPER.md` says what is inferred and which conditions it puts on the platform.
- **The fence-object control in the fallback shape.** When the platform applies the fence part with one GitOps identity that the projects share, that identity must be a writer, and the policy `br-sandbox-fence` then does not stop a project from writing a fence object through it. The control holds only if the project and the platform act under different identities, or if the GitOps project of the project refuses it the fence kinds. The chart cannot see which case the cluster is in (`POLICIES.md`, section Fence objects).
- **A project can declare any image in its own pool.** The pod policy compares the image of the agent with the `image` of the resource, as written. It says "this pool declared it", not "this repository is trusted". `registry_mirror` does not change that image.
- **`imagePullPolicy: Always` on the agent.** The cost is one registry call at each pod start; a start can fail when the registry does not answer (not tested on a cluster).
- **`kubectl debug`** on a sandbox pod is refused for every identity (`BR_SANDBOX_EPHEMERAL_CONTAINERS`); debug a copy in another namespace.
- **The keeper namespace.** With `operator.enabled` the chart renders in the system namespace `br-sandbox-default-deny` and `br-sandbox-egress-keeper`: egress only to `network.api_server_addresses`, `network.store_cidrs` and the cluster DNS. It is not tested on a cluster, and the probes of the kubelet under a default-deny policy depend on the network plugin.
- **The definition.** It has one version, `v1alpha1`, and no conversion: a later change of its schema has no conversion between versions (inferred). With two releases of the chart in one cluster the chart cannot see that one of them sets `crds.install`.
- **The first seconds of a fresh pod.** The network policies bind a fresh pod only once the network plugin enforces them; the window is not closed (README, The enforcement window).
- **The network check in the two-release install.** The check probes the `namespaces` of the release that holds `network_check.enabled`, and it needs `operator.enabled`. In the install of `INSTALL.md` the operator release has `namespaces: []` and the fence release cannot turn the check on, so the check probes no sandbox namespace there: the key `namespaces` of its ConfigMap is empty, no probe pod starts, and the Role, RoleBinding and ConfigMap `br-sandbox-netcheck` are not rendered in any sandbox namespace. In the two-release install, chart 0.4.0 runs no network check of a sandbox namespace. In that shape nothing of the chart proves on the cluster that the network plugin enforces the policies of a sandbox namespace. The fence check of the keeper proves the names and the shape, not the enforcement. The one route that needs no change of the chart is a dedicated probe namespace in the `namespaces` of the operator release, in four steps (`INSTALL.md`, Network check of a sandbox namespace; tested offline: `check_the_network_check_covers_the_namespaces_of_its_release` and `check_the_netcheck_route_renders_the_check_of_one_probe_namespace`). A completed Job of the operator release with `namespaces: []` (for example `br-sandbox-netcheck-initial` of the fixture values) exits 0 and proves nothing for any sandbox namespace. The probe namespace holds one `SandboxPool` at 0 replicas for the time of the check. When this route has been run on a cluster and the Job reports no mismatch, it proves on that cluster that the network plugin enforces the default-deny policy of the chart, and that no rule of the cluster opens a namespace with this name. This repository records no such run at this version. A probe reads `refused` for any non-zero exit of `nc` (reset, drop, a name that does not resolve), and the targets are the classes that the script discovers plus `network_check.targets`, so the statement holds for the targets of the Job and no wider. It does not probe a namespace of a project that `namespaces` does not list, or a fence that the stamper made. For an entry of `namespaces` in the mode `allow` or `full` (the install values may list some), the Job proves the refusal of the targets that it probes there; for an entry in mode `allow` when `egress.enabled` is `true`, it also proves the refusal of a `CONNECT` through the proxy to each target and to the off-list host. It does not prove the allowed traffic of the modes `allow` and `full`, and the checks of that traffic on a real cluster are not this Job.
- **The proxied modes.** The modes `allow` and `full` with a credential set need inputs that a project would choose and that widen its egress: the allow list, the credential set, the proxy address and the proxy CA. In 0.4.0 they are values of the platform on the entry of `namespaces`. The stamper has no rule for them, and the namespace policy grants them only through `grants.modes`. Who gives the allow list and who approves it is not decided.
- **A person who takes control of the keeper.** The keeper has no `pods` create right (`rbac.yaml`, rule of the controller): the identity that creates the pods is the keeper through its ReplicaSet and the runner identity directly (`pods` create), so the same rules bound both. A person who takes control of either can start in a sandbox namespace pods of the image of the pool with another command, other environment variables, another DNS setting, another node selector, another pull policy, another grace period, another `runAsGroup`, another `readOnlyRootFilesystem`, other exact init-container resources, `envFrom` or ConfigMap volumes by name (any ConfigMap of the namespace) and `hostAliases`: no rule judges them. The network policy, the quota, the image rule, the service-account rule and the host and privilege rules still hold.
- **Placement and priority.** The pod policy does not judge the placement and the priority of a pod (`nodeName`, tolerations, affinity, `priorityClassName`, `schedulerName`), and it does not refuse a container with another name. A person who takes control of the keeper, or of an identity that creates pods in a sandbox namespace, can choose the node of a pod, make a pod tolerate a taint, ask a priority class that exists in the cluster, and add a container of the tools repository. The effect can go out of the namespace: a pod of another project can be removed for a pod of higher priority. The quota, the network policy and the other rules of the pod policy still hold. Open for the next chart version: two traps for a rule (the API server sets defaults for tolerations, the scheduler name and possibly a priority before the policy reads the pod; a value of the chart for one of these fields must stay allowed). TO DECIDE (owner of the platform): rules in the next chart version, or a control of the platform on these fields until then.
- **Sysctls.** A pod that sets a sysctl outside the 12 names of the safe list is refused (`BR_SANDBOX_SYSCTLS`): `kernel.shm_rmid_forced`, `net.ipv4.ip_local_port_range`, `net.ipv4.ip_unprivileged_port_start`, `net.ipv4.tcp_syncookies`, `net.ipv4.ping_group_range`, `net.ipv4.ip_local_reserved_ports`, `net.ipv4.tcp_keepalive_time`, `net.ipv4.tcp_fin_timeout`, `net.ipv4.tcp_keepalive_intvl`, `net.ipv4.tcp_keepalive_probes`, `net.ipv4.tcp_rmem`, `net.ipv4.tcp_wmem`. The safe list is the one up to Kubernetes 1.32, stricter than the newest Baseline list.
- **Pod Security labels.** The pod policy does not depend on the Pod Security labels of the namespace. The owner of a namespace that the chart does not render should set them as a second barrier.
- **The image of the other containers is a repository bound, not a constant.** The agent container runs the image of the `SandboxPool`. Every other container (tools, seed, init) passes with any tag or digest of the tools repository, and of the check repository when the network check is on; the chart does not compare the digest that it renders.
- **One maker for one namespace.** One namespace has one maker of its fence: a fence release, or the stamper. The stamper acts on every scope of the scope table, and also on namespaces that exist. Do not turn the stamper on while a fence release fences a namespace of a scope of the table. The effect is not tested: the stamper can take the objects, overwrite them, or delete them when it is turned off. Not tested: to turn the stamper off, or to remove the policy engine, while stamped namespaces exist. The policy engine can then delete the fence objects that it made; a namespace with no fence gets no pool. Remove the pools first. See `STAMPER.md`.
- **Rights of the policy engine.** The stamper needs rights that the chart does not render and that the owner of the platform grants to the background controller of the policy engine (`STAMPER.md`, item 3: the ClusterRole, the order, and the refusal of `helm upgrade`). With these rights, a person who takes control of the background controller of the policy engine can write service accounts, quotas, limit ranges, ConfigMaps, RoleBindings and NetworkPolicies in every namespace, and can bind the two ClusterRoles of the sandbox there.
- **An allow entry in a private or link-local range.** An entry of `allow.cidrs` inside a private or link-local range is not refused by the chart. Not tested: which of the allow entry and the deny list of the proxy wins for such an address.
- **Keeper behaviour that a user of a small pool meets.** With a pool that has `scale_down_step` warm pods or fewer (the default step is 5), one edit of the image or of the size that the namespace refuses removes all the warm pods of the pool. The pool shows `PodsRefused` and makes pods again when the edit is corrected. A claimed pod is never removed.
