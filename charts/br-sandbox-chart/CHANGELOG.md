# Changelog

## 0.4.0

Breaking minor. The chart has two parts: an operator part, installed once per cluster, and a fence part, applied to each namespace by its name and mode. A pool is a `SandboxPool` resource that the keeper reads; the chart no longer renders a ReplicaSet. Chart 0.4.x needs keeper 0.2.x, and chart 0.3.x needs keeper 0.1.x: keeper 0.2 refuses a document with `pools` at start.

- No upgrade path from 0.3.x: remove a 0.3.x release before the install. An upgrade in place was not tested.
- Values: `keeper.*` becomes `operator.*`. A key `keeper` fails the render with `BR_SANDBOX_KEEPER_VALUES_REMOVED{key=keeper}`. `clients` becomes the list `namespaces` (name and mode) and the platform table `scopes`; a key `clients` fails the render with `BR_SANDBOX_CLIENTS_REMOVED{key=clients}`. The `client_id` and the runner identity of a namespace come from the scope that matches its name, never from a label.
- Values moved: `pool.size` becomes `limits.size` and `pool.max_sandboxes` becomes `limits.pods`; the schema refuses `pool.size` and `pool.max_sandboxes` (and `pool.floor`, `pool.refill_seconds` and `pool.margin`). `pool.terminationGracePeriodSeconds` and `pool.node_selector` stay. The quota holds `limits.pods` plus `quota.extra_pods`.
- Namespace names and keys of an entry: the name of a sandbox namespace was `{installation.namespace_prefix}-{client}-{mode}` and is now `namespaces[].name`; the `pool_id` of the adapter document was `{client}-{mode}` and is now the name of the namespace; `allow`, `credential_set` and `proxy`, which were keys of `clients.<client>.modes.<mode>`, are now keys of the entry of `namespaces`.
- Render faults with changed parameters (the fault codes are the same; the parameters name the namespace or the scope, no longer the client): `BR_SANDBOX_ALLOW_EMPTY{namespace=...,mode=...}`, `BR_SANDBOX_ALLOW_MODE{namespace=...,mode=...}`, `BR_SANDBOX_CREDENTIAL_SET_MODE{namespace=...,mode=...}`, `BR_SANDBOX_EGRESS_DISABLED{namespace=...,mode=...,key=...}`, `BR_SANDBOX_NAME_TOO_LONG{namespace=...,egress=...}`, `BR_SANDBOX_PROXY_ADDRESS_DUPLICATE{first=...,second=...,cluster_ip=...}`, `BR_SANDBOX_RUNNER_IS_POD_IDENTITY{scope=...,namespace=...}` and `BR_SANDBOX_SHARED_NAMESPACE{first=...,second=...,namespace=...}` (0.3.0 had `client`, `client_a`, `client_b` and `mode` parameters). `BR_SANDBOX_MISSING_VALUE{key=clients.%s.modes}` is gone with `clients`.
- Values added: `limits.size` and `limits.pods`, `namespaces`, `scopes`, `render_namespace` (default `false`: the chart renders no managed Namespace unless it is `true`), `grants.writers` (default `[]`) and `grants.modes` (default `{}`), `crds.install`, `stamper.enabled` (default `false`) and `stamper.identity` (default `system:serviceaccount:kyverno:kyverno-background-controller`; it must be in `grants.writers`), and the block `operator`.
- Default changed (from `true`): `network_check.enabled` is `false` by default, and the network check needs `operator.enabled`.
- Removed: the ReplicaSet (the keeper makes `warm-{namespace}` from the `SandboxPool` of the namespace); the root key `image`; the per-mode `pool` and `runner`; `pool.floor`, `pool.max_sandboxes`, `pool.refill_seconds` and `pool.margin`. The per-namespace pod policies and bindings `{namespace}-pod` of 0.3.0 are gone: one policy `br-sandbox-pod` serves every managed namespace.
- Changed: every NetworkPolicy of the chart is named with the prefix `br-sandbox-`. The rights of the controller and of the runner are the ClusterRoles `br-sandbox-controller` and `br-sandbox-runner`, bound in each namespace by a RoleBinding. The image of the agent and its size are bound by the `SandboxPool` of the namespace of the pod and by `limits.size`, and no longer by the chart values.
- Added: the `SandboxPool` definition (`crds.install`, default `false`); the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-pod-template` (key `pod-template.json`) of each namespace; the pod policy `br-sandbox-pod` with the bindings `br-sandbox-pod` and `br-sandbox-pod-update`; the namespace policy `br-sandbox-namespace`; the fence-object policy `br-sandbox-fence`; the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-grants`; the optional stamper (`stamper.enabled`, default `false`); in the system namespace the policies `br-sandbox-default-deny` and `br-sandbox-egress-keeper`.
- Pod policy at an update: in 0.3.0 every rule ran again at an update, with the bounds of the chart values. In 0.4.0 the rules that read the `SandboxPool` judge a creation only (`BR_SANDBOX_IMAGE`, and the bound of the agent size by the pool in `BR_SANDBOX_RESOURCES`; both put `request.operation != 'CREATE' ||` before the first read of `params`), and the other rules still judge the updated pod. `BR_SANDBOX_IMMUTABLE` now also compares the images, the init containers, the ephemeral containers and `serviceAccountName`. The binding `br-sandbox-pod-update` matches the update of `pods`, `pods/ephemeralcontainers` and `pods/resize`, and has no `paramRef`. Tested offline with the policy model (`test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`); not tested on a cluster.
- Render faults, new in 0.4.0: `BR_SANDBOX_LINK_LOCAL_MISSING{key=network.internal_cidrs,range=169.254.0.0/16}` (a list in `network.internal_cidrs` with no entry that is or covers `169.254.0.0/16`); `BR_SANDBOX_NOTHING_TO_RENDER` (both parts off, no `crds.install`, no entry); `BR_SANDBOX_OPERATOR_PART_REQUIRED{key=network_check}` and `{key=stamper}`; `BR_SANDBOX_OPERATOR_SETTING_REMOVED{key=pools}`; `BR_SANDBOX_NO_SCOPE{namespace=...}` and `BR_SANDBOX_SCOPES_OVERLAP{scope=...,other=...}`; `BR_SANDBOX_MODE_UNKNOWN{namespace=...,mode=...}` (0.3.0 named the client, not the namespace); `BR_SANDBOX_MODE_NOT_STAMPED{namespace=...,mode=...}`; `BR_SANDBOX_STAMPER_NO_SCOPES`, `BR_SANDBOX_STAMPER_NOT_A_WRITER{identity=...}` and `BR_SANDBOX_STAMPER_RULE_NAME_TOO_LONG{rule=...}`. `BR_SANDBOX_POOL_INVALID` is gone with the pool keys. The admission rule codes of the namespace policy are `BR_SANDBOX_MODE_UNKNOWN`, `BR_SANDBOX_MODE_NOT_GRANTED`, `BR_SANDBOX_LABEL_IMMUTABLE` and `BR_SANDBOX_SCOPE`, and the code of the fence-object policy is `BR_SANDBOX_FENCE`.
- Admission rules: 21 rules in all. Rule 14 (`BR_SANDBOX_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`) requires the ServiceAccount `sandbox`; rule 15 (`BR_SANDBOX_EPHEMERAL_CONTAINERS`) refuses ephemeral containers, so `kubectl debug` on a sandbox pod is refused. Rules 16 to 21 hold the Baseline level of the Pod Security Standards in the policy itself: `BR_SANDBOX_PROBE_HOST` (no `host` in `httpGet` or `tcpSocket` of a probe or a lifecycle hook: the kubelet sends such a request from the node, where no network policy applies), `BR_SANDBOX_PROC_MOUNT` (`procMount` `Default` or unset), `BR_SANDBOX_APPARMOR` (no profile outside `RuntimeDefault` and `Localhost`, no annotation other than `runtime/default` or `localhost/*`), `BR_SANDBOX_SELINUX` (no user, no role, a type of the Baseline list), `BR_SANDBOX_SYSCTLS` (the safe sysctls of the Baseline level) and `BR_SANDBOX_WINDOWS_HOST_PROCESS` (no `hostProcess` true). A pod that the chart renders is admitted by all of them. They run on both bindings and read no parameter. Tested offline with the policy model (`test-sandbox-chart-baseline.sh`); not tested on a cluster.
- The keeper document and the adapter document name the agent container `agent` (`agent_container`, required by the keeper). The agent container of the pod template has `imagePullPolicy: Always`: the cost is one registry call at each pod start, and the limit is that a start can fail when the registry does not answer (not tested on a cluster).
- `registry_mirror` does not apply to the image of a pool: the agent of a pod runs exactly the `image` of its `SandboxPool`, as written.
- Not proved offline, and not tested on a cluster: that the CEL of the three policies compiles, that the API server takes the `SandboxPool` as the parameter of the pod policy, the grants and the fence-object control, the stamper, and the rights on the API server. A cluster run holds them; none is recorded at this version.
- Tests: the offline suite checks each sentence above (`test-sandbox-chart-operator-docs.sh`) and the objects, the rights, the policies and the stamper (`test-sandbox-chart-operator-parts.sh`, `-operator-rights.sh`, `-operator-template.sh`, `-operator-crd.sh`, `-pod-policy.sh`, `-namespace-policy.sh`, `-fence-policy.sh`, `-stamper.sh`, `-scopes.sh`). The lint loop of the CI covers the new fixtures.
- Network check in the two-release install: the check probes the `namespaces` of the release that holds `network_check.enabled`, so the install of `INSTALL.md` runs no network check of a sandbox namespace. One route needs no change of the chart, in four steps (`INSTALL.md`): an upgrade of the operator release with a copy of `ci/netcheck-route-values.yaml` (one probe namespace `<scope>-netcheck` in mode `none`, the scope being a key of the table `scopes` of the install values, `render_namespace: true`, `network_check.enabled: false`, so no Job fails before a pool exists); a `SandboxPool` in that namespace (`minReplicas: 0`, `maxReplicas: 1`, `margin: 0`); an upgrade with a copy of `ci/netcheck-route-run-values.yaml` added (`network_check.enabled: true`, a new `run_id`, at least one `network_check.targets` entry), which renders the Job `br-sandbox-netcheck-{run_id}`; the deletion of the pool, then an upgrade without the route files. Each step is rendered and linted offline; no step was run on a cluster by the tests of this repository.
- Link-local range: `network.internal_cidrs` replaces the default list, so a list with no entry that is or covers `169.254.0.0/16` now fails the render with `BR_SANDBOX_LINK_LOCAL_MISSING{key=network.internal_cidrs,range=169.254.0.0/16}`. The policies `br-sandbox-egress-full` and `br-sandbox-egress-proxy` and the upstream deny list of the proxy are built from that one value. Reported by the operator of a test cluster, no record in this repository: with a list that had no such range, `169.254.169.254:80` was open from a pod of mode `full`. An entry counts as a cover only if it is a well-formed IPv4 CIDR (four decimal octets of 0 to 255 and a prefix of 0 to 32); a malformed entry covers nothing. An install whose `network.internal_cidrs` was written without that range is refused after the upgrade: the owner adds `169.254.0.0/16`, or an entry that covers it, to the list. A render with the default list, or a list that holds the range, is unchanged. A change of `network.internal_cidrs` changes the proxy configuration, so the upgrade rolls the proxy Deployments of the egress namespaces (the annotation `br.sandbox/proxy-config-sha256` changes).
- Stamper conditions (`STAMPER.md`): the rights that the policy engine needs to generate the fence objects are not rendered by the chart and are needed (reported by the operator of a test cluster; no record in this repository; not proved here), and one namespace has one maker of its fence: a fence release, or the stamper.
- A warm pod that is Pending and that the scheduler marks Unschedulable is held by keeper 0.2: it has no age deadline and is not deleted at `warm_max_age_seconds` (keeper 0.1.x deleted it). Stated by the keeper 0.2 text; not tested by this chart.
- Keeper 0.2.0: the file `files/sandboxpool-crd.yaml` equals, byte for byte, the definition file of keeper 0.2.0. The Rust tests `chart_definition.rs` and `chart_rights.rs` of the keeper crate compare the definition, the rules of the roles of the keeper identity and the NetworkPolicy names that the chart renders with the exports of keeper 0.2.0 (the definition file, `RIGHTS`, `NETWORK_POLICY_NAMES`), and `chart_names.rs` compares the name of the policy and of the binding `br-sandbox-pod`, of the ResourceQuota, of the template ConfigMap and its data key with the constants of the keeper (`ADMISSION_BINDING_NAME`, `QUOTA_NAME`, `POD_TEMPLATE_CONFIGMAP`, `POD_TEMPLATE_KEY`). The fixtures of `ci/` name the image of keeper 0.2.0 by digest.

## 0.3.0

Added: the chart value `exec_timeout_seconds`, default `600`, an integer above zero. The adapter document (the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-config` of each sandbox namespace, key `config.json`) gains one key, `exec_timeout_seconds`, and every document holds it.

- The key `exec_timeout_seconds` is the bound, in seconds, of one `run` in the foreground; the same key bounds the seed copy at an open. A command that needs longer uses the background form of `run`, which returns the id of the process and not its output. This is read from the code of the adapter and is not tested here or on a cluster. The default `600` (ten minutes) is the choice of the chart.
- Before 0.3.0 the document had no such key, and the adapter, read from its code, then applied no bound to these two operations. With 0.3.0 each document holds the value: a values file that sets nothing renders `600`.
- Effect of an upgrade from 0.2.x: foreground commands that ran with no bound under 0.2.x (read from the code of the adapter, as above) are cut after 600 seconds by default; a runner that needs longer raises `exec_timeout_seconds`.
- A value that is not an integer above zero is refused: `0`, a negative number, a string and a float by the schema, `null` (which removes the key) by the new render fault `BR_SANDBOX_EXEC_TIMEOUT_INVALID{key=exec_timeout_seconds}`.
- The README no longer lists `exec_timeout_seconds` among the keys that the chart has no value for.
- Changed right: the runner Role `br-sandbox-runner` of each sandbox namespace gains `get` on `pods/exec`. Versions 0.2.x rendered `create` only on `pods/exec`; 0.3.0 renders `create` and `get`. An upgrade from 0.2.x so grants one more verb to the runner service account. Every other rule of the runner Role is unchanged, and no other Role, ClusterRole, binding or admission rule changes. Reason: the adapter uses a WebSocket (read from the code of the adapter), and a WebSocket handshake is a GET request, for which the API server asks `get` on `pods/exec` and, on Kubernetes 1.35 or later while the feature gate `AuthorizePodWebsocketUpgradeCreatePermission` is on (it is on by default from 1.35), `create` as well. The Kubernetes behaviour in this sentence is read from public pages (the Kubernetes feature gates reference, KEP 4006 of kubernetes/enhancements, the issue kubernetes/kubernetes#78741) and is not tested on a cluster; the README names the pages with their addresses and gives the wording.
- Added: the default of `network_check.image`, `ghcr.io/botresources/br-sandbox-check@sha256:51bd276e6c14b568a55633419d31a824338d423360c08d6584ecf28a7bcf556d`: the image `br-sandbox-check`, by the digest of a manifest list for `linux/amd64` and `linux/arm64` (read from the registry listing of that digest). Before, the value had no default, and a render with `network_check.enabled` and no image failed with `BR_SANDBOX_MISSING_VALUE{key=network_check.image}`; now a values file that sets no image renders the default, and a project value replaces it. A `null` value removes the key and still gives that fault. What the image holds (an Alpine base and `kubectl`, both pinned, and the ten programs of the check script) is read from the README of the image and is not tested here; no cluster has run the check Job with this image. The offline suite checks that the Job, the CronJob and each probe pod render the default and the project value, that the README and this entry hold the digest of `values.yaml`, and their sentences (`test-sandbox-chart-netcheck-image.sh`).
- Tests: the offline suite checks the rules of the runner Role against the table, with `pods/exec` at `create` and `get`, and the README and CHANGELOG sentences on it (`test-sandbox-chart-runner-role.sh`, `test-sandbox-chart-runner-role-docs.sh`); the assertion of `test-sandbox-chart-rbac.sh` that held `pods/exec:create` now holds `pods/exec:create,get`; the offline suite checks the key, the refusals and the sentences of the README and of this entry (`test-sandbox-chart-exec-timeout.sh`, `test-sandbox-chart-exec-timeout-docs.sh`); the lint loop of the CI covers the new overlay `ci/exec-timeout-values.yaml`; the test `crates/br-runner-sandbox/tests/chart_exec_timeout.rs` renders the chart on each fixture, loads each document through `SandboxConfigInput` and `SandboxConfig::try_from`, and checks that the adapter holds the rendered value (`600` by default, `1800` with the new overlay).

## 0.2.4

The runner can read its document: the chart now renders the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-config` of each sandbox namespace in the flat keys and the format that `SandboxConfigInput` of `br-runner-sandbox` reads. Before, the chart wrote nested YAML (`pool`, `tools`, `save`, `store`, `idle_ttl`, `client`, `mode`) under the key `config.yaml`; the adapter reads JSON, and it silently ignores a key that it does not know, so the nested keys were never read and the required keys were missing.

- The ConfigMap `br-sandbox-config` of each sandbox namespace keeps its name (the Role `br-sandbox-runner` has `get` on that name only, and the Role is not changed) and holds one JSON document under `config.json`. The key `config.yaml` is removed. The system namespace holds no ConfigMap `br-sandbox-config` (0.2.2 removed it; it holds `br-sandbox-keeper-config`).
- Keys: `namespace`, `client_id`, `pool_id` (`{client}-{mode}`) and `network_mode` (`none`, `allow`, `full`) from the (client, mode); `label_prefix`; `save_ttl_seconds` (`save.ttl_seconds`), `save_scope_paths` (`save.paths`), `restore_timeout_seconds`, `idle_ttl_seconds` (`idle_ttl`), `idle_keep_min_state_bytes`; `agent_container`, `seed_container` (`tools.seed_container.name`), `tools_path` (`{tools.mount_path}/bin/bsdtar`), `seed_tools_path`, `seed_root`; `store_endpoint`, `store_bucket`, `store_region`, `store_path_style`; and, in mode `allow` only, `allowlist` (`allow.domains` then `allow.cidrs`, the lists of the proxy). `label_prefix` is a constant (`br.sandbox`), the same in both documents; the store keys, the save age, the idle time and the idle threshold are each one chart value, rendered into the keeper document and the adapter document.
- `agent_container` is `agent`, the name of the agent container of the pool pod. The adapter default is `sandbox`, so a document without the key would have named a container that the pod does not have. The agent container name, the seed root (`/skills`) and the seed tools path (`/sbx/bin/bsdtar`) are now one template each, used by the pod template and by the document. With `tools.seed_container.enabled` false the pod has no seed container, and the document holds neither `seed_container` nor `seed_tools_path`; the adapter then uses its defaults `seed` and `/sbx/bin/bsdtar`; the failure of an open with a non-empty seed follows from the adapter code and is not tested here or on a cluster, and the same holds for the statement that an open with an empty seed is not affected.
- `save.paths` is rendered as given. The adapter refuses, when it starts, a list that does not hold `/work`, a list in which one path holds or equals another, a list in which a path holds or is held by `/skills`, and a path with a `..` part; the chart does not check these rules, and the README says so.
- Not in the document: `store_credentials` (the credential comes from a Secret of the runner namespace, and the importer adds it; the chart renders no Secret), `kube_context`, and the keys that no chart value feeds (`save_zstd_level`, `open_timeout_seconds`, `exec_timeout_seconds`, `save_timeout_seconds`, `claim_max_attempts`, `cold_create`): the adapter uses its default. The warm age, the tombstone age and the pool keys (`floor`, `max_sandboxes`, `refill_seconds`, `margin`) are not in the adapter document; the adapter never read them, and they stay in the keeper document.
- Upgrade from 0.2.3: a runner that read `config.yaml` finds no such key. The runner identity (Role `br-sandbox-runner`, bound to `runner.service_account` of `runner.namespace`) is the only reader of the ConfigMap; no pod mounts it, and it holds no secret.
- New render fault `BR_SANDBOX_ALLOW_EMPTY{client=...,mode=allow}`: with `egress.enabled`, a mode `allow` with no `allow.domains` and no `allow.cidrs` fails the render, because the adapter refuses that document when it starts. This changes what 0.2.0 to 0.2.3 accepted: they rendered such a namespace, and 0.2.4 refuses it. A namespace that denies everything is mode `none`. A values file of 0.2.3 that enables egress and holds such a namespace must give it a domain or a cidr, or change its mode. With `egress.enabled` false no list can be given (`BR_SANDBOX_EGRESS_DISABLED`), so a mode `allow` is still rendered with no list; its document has no `allowlist` and the adapter refuses it, so that namespace cannot be used until egress is enabled and a list is given.
- Fix in the README: the sentence on the network check said that a probe is tried again every second for at most `network_check.enforcement_wait_seconds`. The limit is checked between two tries, never during one: under a policy that drops the packets a try waits up to 2 seconds for its `nc -w 2` timeout, and a sleep of 1 second comes between a check below the limit and the next try, so a probe can go on up to 3 seconds after the limit, and a refusal in that time reads as `refused` with a `refused_after_seconds` above `limit_seconds`; the printed times are whole seconds (`date +%s`). The paragraph on the Job failure now says the same (a probe that is still reached when the limit is checked). The 0.2.3 text does not change.
- Fixed: the network check created the probe pod `br-sandbox-netcheck-probe` of each sandbox namespace by a fixed name and deleted it at the end with no wait, so a pod left by an earlier Job that was killed made the next Job fail on `AlreadyExists`. The Job now deletes a leftover pod of that name in the namespace and waits for it to be gone (at most 60 seconds) before it creates the probe pod. A leftover pod that the delete does not remove (the delete command fails for any cause) gives one object with `kind` `probe_pod`, `expected` `absent` and `observed` `not_deleted` for that namespace, the Job makes no probe in it and fails. The Role of the check identity is not changed, and the check image needs no new program.
- Tests: the offline suite checks the document (`test-sandbox-chart-adapter-config.sh`), the new render fault (`test-sandbox-chart-egress.sh`), the new README sentences (`test-sandbox-chart-netcheck-contract.sh`), the 3 second bound (`test-sandbox-chart-netcheck-window.sh`) and the leftover probe pod (`test-sandbox-chart-netcheck-leftover.sh`). The checks of the shared values (`test-sandbox-chart-keeper-values.sh`) and the test `crates/br-sandbox-keeper/tests/chart_values.rs` read the flat adapter keys; the pool keys, the warm age and the tombstone age are checked in the keeper document only. The test `crates/br-runner-sandbox/tests/chart_config.rs` renders the chart on each fixture and loads the document of each sandbox namespace through `SandboxConfigInput` and `SandboxConfig::try_from`, and checks that the render fault stops a mode `allow` with no list when egress is enabled.

## 0.2.3

Fix: the network check waited for no enforcement. On a cluster with flannel and the kube-router network policy controller, the policy started some seconds after a fresh probe pod: a probe at once reached 2 of 2 targets, the same probe 30 seconds later reached 0, so the Job failed although the policy worked.

- A direct TCP probe of the probe pod is tried again every second until it is refused, for at most `network_check.enforcement_wait_seconds` (new key, integer from 1 to 999999999, default 60), counted from the start of the probe script in the pod; the limit is one budget for the whole pod, a later target gets what is left of it, and once it is spent each remaining target is tried once. Still reached at the limit: the probe is `reached` and the Job fails. The positive controls and the `CONNECT` probes are not tried again.
- The `tcp` result object has two new fields: `refused_after_seconds` (the seconds from the start of the probe script to the first refusal, or the text `not refused`) and `limit_seconds`. No field was removed or renamed.
- The Job waits for each probe pod for 180 seconds plus the limit, and its `activeDeadlineSeconds` (also in the CronJob) is 1800 plus the limit for each sandbox namespace (it was 1800).
- The check image needs `sleep` and `date` besides the programs of 0.2.0; a missing `sleep` or `date` gives a `precondition` row like a missing `nc`.
- `refused_after_seconds` is the time from the start of the script of the whole probe pod, not of one target; the `nc -w 2` wait of each dropped probe counts in it, so under a policy that drops packets the k-th target reports about 2 times k seconds, and under a policy that resets the connection a probe refused at once reports about 0. The README says so.
- `refused` means that `nc` exited with any non-zero status (a reset, the timeout of a dropped probe, a name that does not resolve, an `nc` that fails). At the start of the script, before any probe, a missing `nc`, `sleep` or `date` (the first one missing, in that order; `target` is its name) or a `NETCHECK_ENFORCEMENT_WAIT` that is not an integer from 1 to 999999999 prints one object with the new `kind` `precondition` (`observed` `missing` or `invalid`, `control` `not_applicable`) and exits with status 2. An `nc` that exists but is broken is caught only by the positive control of the Job pod, which runs the same image as the probe pods.
- The README no longer words the policy rules of the sandbox namespaces as a guarantee for a fresh pod: they hold once the network plugin enforces them.
- A window exists in which a fresh pod reaches internal targets; the chart does not close it, and the report does not tell its length: `refused_after_seconds` is the time from the start of the probe script to the first refusal of the probe, and it carries the `nc -w 2` waits of the probes dropped before it. Measured with flannel and kube-router only; not reproduced by the offline suite.

## 0.2.2

The keeper pod can start: the chart now renders the keeper a document in the keys and the format that the keeper reads. Before, the chart wrote YAML with keys such as `store`, `lease`, `settings`, `save` and `system_namespace` at `/etc/br-sandbox/config.yaml`, and the keeper refuses every unknown key and reads JSON.

- New ConfigMap `br-sandbox-keeper-config` of the system namespace (only with `keeper.enabled`), key `config.json`: one JSON document with keys of the keeper only. `BR_SANDBOX_CONFIG` is `/etc/br-sandbox/config.json` and the volume `config` mounts that ConfigMap. The ConfigMap `br-sandbox-config` of the system namespace is removed: no component read it. The ConfigMaps `br-sandbox-config` of the sandbox namespaces (the adapter) are unchanged.
- Mapping: `store.{endpoint,bucket,region,path_style}` to `store_endpoint`, `store_bucket`, `store_region`, `store_path_style`; `save.ttl_seconds` to `save_ttl_seconds`; `save.tombstone_ttl_days` to `tombstone_ttl_seconds`; `warm_max_age_seconds`; `keeper.lease.*` to `lease_duration_seconds`, `renew_deadline_seconds`, `retry_period_seconds`; `label_prefix` (`br.sandbox`) and `managed_namespace_selector` (`br.sandbox/managed=true`); each pool of each (client, mode) to an entry of `pools` with `id`, `namespace`, `replicaset`, `min_replicas` (`floor`), `max_replicas` (`max_sandboxes`), `refill_seconds`, `margin`, `idle_ttl_seconds` (`idle_ttl`) and `idle_keep_min_state_bytes`. Every value that the keeper and the adapter share is one value, rendered into both documents; the lease keys, the port and the selector are in the keeper document only. The store credential is not in the document.

The pod targets leave out the pods labelled `br.sandbox/role=proxy`. The status code is the second field of the status line, compared as a whole field (`HTTP/1.1 200 x 403 y` is read as 200); a status line with no three-digit code is `no_result`. Captured on the pinned proxy image (0.61.0), mode `allow`: an IP address that is not on the list and an internal IP address both got 403 at the `CONNECT`; a name that is not on the list was not captured at the `CONNECT`. On a deployed cluster the Job therefore does not prove the pivot barriers of mode `full`; that proof, a test server at an internal address that records its connections and sees none after a request through the proxy, is to be made on a test cluster; no such test exists in this repository at this version and none was executed.
