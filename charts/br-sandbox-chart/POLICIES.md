# Admission policies of br-sandbox-chart

## Pod policy

With `operator.enabled` the chart renders one `ValidatingAdmissionPolicy` for pods, `br-sandbox-pod`, and two bindings, `br-sandbox-pod` and `br-sandbox-pod-update`. They are cluster-scoped objects and their number does not depend on the number of namespaces: both bindings select the namespaces by the label `br.sandbox/managed=true` and name no namespace (a test renders the policy with one and with several entries). The agent image and the agent size are not chart values of a namespace: the bound of the image and of the size of the agent container is the `SandboxPool` of the namespace of the pod, and the maximum of a container is `limits.size`, one value of the platform. The expressions are inlined from the chart values (platform maximum, runtime class, tools and check repositories).

- **Create.** The binding `br-sandbox-pod` matches the creation of pods. Its parameter is the `SandboxPool` of the namespace of the pod: `paramRef` has `selector: {}`, no name and no namespace, and `parameterNotFoundAction: Deny` with `failurePolicy: Fail`. A managed namespace with no `SandboxPool` admits no new pod, the probe pod of the network check included (the network check needs a pool in the namespace that it checks). With two resources in one namespace a pod must pass both. Read from the pages on `ParamRef` and on the policy (Kubernetes documentation): `selector: {}` matches all resources of the kind, several matches are ANDed, the namespace of the admitted object is used when `namespace` is unset, and `Deny` makes the binding apply the `failurePolicy`. Not tested on a cluster: that the API server compiles the policy with the definition as the parameter kind and reads `params.spec.image` and `params.spec.size`.
- **Update.** The binding `br-sandbox-pod-update` matches the update of `pods`, of `pods/ephemeralcontainers` and of `pods/resize`, and has no `paramRef`: `params` is then null (read from the policy page). It exists because the refusal for a missing parameter applies to a whole binding (read from the `ParamRef` page: "the binding enforces the `failurePolicy`"; not tested on a cluster), so one binding could not refuse a new pod without a resource and still accept the patches of an admitted pod. The two rules that read `params` (`BR_SANDBOX_RESOURCES` and `BR_SANDBOX_IMAGE`) put `request.operation != 'CREATE' ||` before the first read, and a test fails when any expression reads `params` before that guard. An update of a pod is never judged against the resource: a patch of `br.sandbox/` labels and annotations of an admitted pod is accepted after a change of `spec.image` of the resource and after the delete of the resource (`test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`, policy model). What an update cannot change, with a resource or without: `BR_SANDBOX_IMMUTABLE` compares the containers (images, resources, security contexts, ports and env included), the init containers, the ephemeral containers, `hostUsers`, `hostNetwork`, `hostPID`, `hostIPC`, `shareProcessNamespace`, `serviceAccountName`, `automountServiceAccountToken`, `runtimeClassName`, the pod `securityContext` and the volumes. The Kubernetes page on Pods says an update may change `spec.containers[*].image`, `spec.initContainers[*].image`, `spec.activeDeadlineSeconds`, `spec.terminationGracePeriodSeconds`, `spec.tolerations` and `spec.schedulingGates`, so an image patch is possible and the policy refuses it (read from the page, not tested on a cluster). The runner holds `patch` on `pods`, so a patch of an image is within reach of a taken-over runner without this rule.
- **Resize.** A resize of a pod changes `spec.containers[*].resources` after the creation. The Kubernetes page on the resize of container resources (read from the page, not tested on a cluster) says that the change is made by an update of the pod subresource `resize` (`kubectl patch pod ... --subresource resize`), that `spec.containers[*].resources` is mutable for cpu and memory, and that the feature is stable since Kubernetes v1.35 and first available in v1.27. The policy and the binding `br-sandbox-pod-update` match `pods/resize`, so `BR_SANDBOX_IMMUTABLE` refuses a resize of a sandbox pod for every identity, with a resource or without (`test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`, policy model); a resize that changes no judged field is admitted. A resize needs the verb `update` or `patch` on `pods/resize`: no Role of the chart holds it. The chart matches no other pod subresource: it does not claim that `pods/status` and `pods/binding` cannot change a field judged at creation, and it does not test them.
- **The two bindings.** Both bindings are written in one template file, `admission-policy.yaml`, under the one condition `operator.enabled`; no value turns one of them off alone, and a test renders every fixture of `ci/` and holds that each one with the pod policy holds both bindings, with the policy name `br-sandbox-pod` and the namespace selector `br.sandbox/managed=true` (`test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`). The fence check of the keeper reads the binding `br-sandbox-pod`. It does not read `br-sandbox-pod-update`. If that binding is absent, an identity with the patch right on pods can change the image of a pod after its admission, and the keeper does not see it. Only an identity with a write right on admission policy bindings at cluster scope can remove a binding: that is the platform. No Role and no ClusterRole of any render of the chart holds such a right, so neither the runner nor the keeper can remove a binding (read from the RBAC objects of the chart; proved by the render test `check_no_role_of_any_render_writes_an_admission_binding` in `test-sandbox-chart-pod-policy.sh`). A project identity is not an object of the chart: that it holds no such right is a condition on the platform, not tested here.
- **Chart 0.3.0 on an update** (read from its template, `admission-policy.yaml` and `_vap.tpl` of that release): one policy and one binding for each namespace, matching `CREATE` and `UPDATE` of `pods` and `UPDATE` of `pods/ephemeralcontainers`. Every rule ran again at an update, with the bounds of the chart values: the size rule and the image rule (a repository prefix with a tag or a digest, so a patch to another tag of an allowed repository passed). `BR_SANDBOX_IMMUTABLE` compared `hostUsers`, the security contexts and the resources of the containers, and the volumes; it did not compare images, init containers, ephemeral containers or the account name.
- **Role.** A pod passes if it is `role=sandbox` and follows the rules below. Any other role, or none, is refused (`BR_SANDBOX_ROLE`), `role=proxy` included: a proxy pod lives in the egress namespace, which the bindings do not select. The role cannot change (`BR_SANDBOX_ROLE_IMMUTABLE`).
- **Rules of a sandbox pod** (15 rules in all, with the two above): `BR_SANDBOX_USERNS_REQUIRED` `hostUsers` false; `BR_SANDBOX_HOST_NAMESPACE` no host network, PID, IPC, port; `BR_SANDBOX_VOLUME_FORBIDDEN` only `emptyDir` and `configMap` volumes, no `secretKeyRef`, no `secretRef`; `BR_SANDBOX_CAPABILITY` drop ALL, add only the seven; `BR_SANDBOX_PRIVILEGE` no privilege, no escalation, seccomp `RuntimeDefault` on the pod and, when a container sets a profile, on that container; `BR_SANDBOX_RESOURCES` cpu, memory and ephemeral-storage requests and limits on every container, none above `limits.size`, and on the container named `agent` none above the `size` of the `SandboxPool` (a field that the resource does not set adds no bound; a non-agent container is bound by `limits.size` only, so the probe pod of the check is not refused by a small pool); `BR_SANDBOX_IMAGE` the container named `agent` runs exactly the `image` of the `SandboxPool` (a resource with no `image` admits no agent), every other container (tools, seed, init, probe) an image of an allowed repository (tools image, check image, each with the mirror) with a tag or a digest; `BR_SANDBOX_TOKEN_OR_RUNTIME` no token, runtime class equals the chart value; `BR_SANDBOX_IMMUTABLE` as above; `BR_SANDBOX_RUN_AS_NON_ROOT` every container of the two lists `containers` and `initContainers` sets `runAsNonRoot: true` and a `runAsUser` above 0 in its own `securityContext`, and the pod does not share its process namespace; `BR_SANDBOX_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` `spec.serviceAccountName` is `sandbox`, with no exception (the pool pod and the probe pod both name it; an absent name is refused); `BR_SANDBOX_EPHEMERAL_CONTAINERS` a sandbox pod has no ephemeral container.
- **Ephemeral containers.** `kubectl debug` on a sandbox pod is refused for every identity: the request to `pods/ephemeralcontainers` carries `spec.ephemeralContainers` and `BR_SANDBOX_EPHEMERAL_CONTAINERS` refuses it (policy model); debug a copy of the pod in another namespace. The Kubernetes page on ephemeral containers (read from the page, not tested on a cluster) says that an ephemeral container cannot set `resources`, `ports` or probes, and that it is added through the `ephemeralcontainers` subresource, which the default `admin` and `edit` roles do not include. The size rule and the image rule read `containers` and `initContainers`, not `ephemeralContainers`, so they do not judge an ephemeral container: the rule on ephemeral containers and `BR_SANDBOX_IMMUTABLE` do. No Role of the chart (runner, keeper, check) holds a verb on `pods/ephemeralcontainers`.
- **Patch rules** (`BR_SANDBOX_LABELS`): a patch changes only `br.sandbox/` keys of labels and annotations; `role` and `pool-id` never change; a claimed pod never leaves `claimed`.
- `BR_SANDBOX_RUN_AS_NON_ROOT` has two exceptions, both exact. The seed container of the regular container list (`containers`) passes by its name (`tools.seed_container.name`) and by its image, which is exactly the tools image that the chart renders (the same string, with the mirror prefix when there is one); it must still set `runAsUser` 0, have a read-only root file system and no port, and `BR_SANDBOX_CAPABILITY` still limits it to the seven capabilities. A container with the seed name and another image is refused. An init container with the seed name, the tools image, uid 0, a read-only root file system and no port is refused too: the exception does not apply to the init container list. The pods of the network check, which have uid 0, pass by the user name of the ServiceAccount that makes them, `system:serviceaccount:{system namespace}:br-sandbox-netcheck`, and by no label or name; they still must not share the process namespace and they still need the account `sandbox`. With `network_check.enabled: false` the rule has no such exception, and with `tools.seed_container.enabled: false` it has no seed exception.

**What the tests prove, and what they do not.** The tests read the rendered expressions: each expression equals an expected text, and a model written in `jq` reads the rendered texts of the rules and the bindings (the operations and resources each binding matches, its `paramRef`, the repositories, the quantities, the guards) and gives a verdict for a created or updated pod and a set of resources. The model is not a CEL evaluator. Not tested on a cluster: that the CEL compiles, that the API server evaluates the expressions as the model does, the refusal and the admission of each case above, the matching of the bindings, and `kubectl debug` on a real pod. The text of each rule is the contract; the model catches a changed bound, a lost guard or a lost binding, not a CEL fault.

**What the policy does not hold.** The seed is written by the adapter through `pods/exec` into the seed container, which runs as root. No rule protects the seed against an adapter that is compromised: it can write any content to `/skills`. The policy holds the shape of the pods, not the content of their files.

0.4.0 is a breaking minor and has no upgrade path from 0.3.x: remove the 0.3.x release first; an upgrade in place was not tested.

A cluster test of the refusals of an unlabelled pod, of a `role=proxy` pod and of a relabel of the role, with the runner service account, in a sandbox namespace, and of the refusal of the non-root rule by the runner service account, is to be made; no such test exists in this repository at this version and none was executed. The CEL of the admission policy is not compiled offline, so at this version it has been neither compiled nor executed on a cluster.

## Mode of a namespace

With `operator.enabled` the chart renders a second policy, `br-sandbox-namespace`, with one binding of the same name, and the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-grants` in the system namespace. They are in the operator part only: a render of the fence part holds none of them. The policy matches the creation and the update of `namespaces` (no subresource), and the binding selects the namespaces by `objectSelector` on the label `br.sandbox/managed=true`, with no namespace selector. Read from the pages on `MatchResources` and on admission webhooks (Kubernetes documentation; not tested on a cluster): for a Namespace the selectors match the labels of the object itself, and `objectSelector` matches when the old object or the new object matches, so a request that adds the managed label and a request that removes it are both matched. The binding takes the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-grants` of the system namespace as parameter, by name and namespace, with `parameterNotFoundAction: Deny` and `failurePolicy: Fail`, so a managed namespace cannot be created or updated while the ConfigMap is absent (`test-sandbox-chart-namespace-policy.sh`). The policy assumes that a project identity has no right to write in the system namespace; the chart does not give that right to anyone and does not test it.

- `BR_SANDBOX_MODE_UNKNOWN`: at creation, the label `br.sandbox/mode` is `none`, `allow` or `full`; no label is refused.
- `BR_SANDBOX_MODE_NOT_GRANTED`: at creation, a mode other than `none` needs the mode in the key `mode.<name of the namespace>` of the grants, or a user in the key `writers`. The grants of a render with no `grants` value hold no mode, so a project gets `none` and nothing else. The grant is read by the name of the namespace; the label of the namespace and its annotations are never read, and a test shows that no rule reads another field than the name and the labels.
- `BR_SANDBOX_LABEL_IMMUTABLE`: at update, the labels `br.sandbox/managed` and `br.sandbox/mode` cannot be added, changed or removed, except by a writer. Removing `managed` would unbind the pod policy, so it is refused too. A request that leaves both labels as they are is admitted, so a namespace whose grant was removed later can still be updated by a project.
- `BR_SANDBOX_SCOPE`: at creation, the name is a scope of the platform (`scopes`) or starts with a scope and a dash, for every identity, writers included. The scopes are copied to the key `scopes` of the grants. A render with no scope refuses every managed creation.

The verdicts are proved by the jq model of the policy tests (`test-sandbox-chart-namespace-policy.sh`): it reads the bindings, the operations, the selector, the parameter and the text of each expression from the render, and applies them to a created and to an updated namespace; mutated renders show that the model changes its verdict when the text changes. It is not a CEL evaluator, and no API server ran these expressions. Read from the CEL page (not tested on a cluster): `split` on a string is a function of the CEL string extension of the API server. The chart does not hold the identity of any writer: the list is a value of the platform. The policy reads the user name only, never a group, so a cluster administrator is a writer only if the list names that user; the chart relies on no group.

## Fence objects

With `operator.enabled` the chart renders a third policy, `br-sandbox-fence`, with one binding of the same name. The binding selects the namespaces by `namespaceSelector` on the label `br.sandbox/managed=true`, takes the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-grants` as parameter by name and namespace, with `parameterNotFoundAction: Deny` and `failurePolicy: Fail`, and names no namespace (`test-sandbox-chart-fence-policy.sh`). The policy matches the creation, the update and the deletion of `networkpolicies`, `resourcequotas`, `limitranges`, `roles`, `rolebindings`, `serviceaccounts` and `configmaps`, and refuses the request unless the user is in the key `writers` of the grants or the namespace is `Terminating`. Of the service accounts only the one named `sandbox` is a fence object, and of the ConfigMaps only those whose name starts with `br-sandbox-`; this includes `br-sandbox-pod-template`, on purpose, because the keeper builds its ReplicaSet from it. A deletion is judged by the name of the old object. The variable `namespaceObject` is listed on the page of the validating admission policy (Kubernetes documentation) as the namespace of the incoming object; that a namespace being deleted has the phase `Terminating` when the controller deletes its objects is not read from a page and not tested on a cluster, so that a deletion of a namespace completes is expected, not proved.

- **Status subresources.** No rule names a subresource (`test-sandbox-chart-fence-policy.sh`). A rule matches a subresource only when the subresource is named: this is expected, not read from a page in this work and not tested on a cluster. The model shows what follows from it: the update of `resourcequotas/status`, which moves the used count of the quota, is not matched, and the update of `resourcequotas` is. A mutated render that names `resourcequotas/status` refuses the controller, so the test sees the change.
- **No grants, no write.** While the ConfigMap `br-sandbox-grants` is absent, every write of a fence object in a managed namespace is refused, by a writer too (model). The chart renders the ConfigMap in the same release as the policy.
- **Limit: one GitOps identity.** The policy tells a writer from a project by the user name and by nothing else. When the platform applies the fence part with the same identity as a project (for example one GitOps controller for both), that identity must be a writer, and then the policy does not stop the project from writing a fence object through it. The control holds only if the project and the platform act under different identities, or if the GitOps project of the project refuses it the fence kinds. The chart cannot see which case the cluster is in; whether an identity can act as another is not described on the pages that were read, and is not tested.
- **Refusal in the tested install.** In the one cluster run reported for this install, a project identity's create of a RoleBinding and its delete of a binding were refused by RBAC, because that identity holds no such right, not by `BR_SANDBOX_FENCE` or another policy code. The refusal by the policy is proved by the model only, and is not proved on a cluster.
- **What the model proves.** `test-sandbox-chart-fence-policy.sh` reads the bindings, the operations, the kinds, the namespace selector, the parameter and the text of the rule from the render and applies them to requests of a project identity, of a writer, of a controller and of a Terminating namespace. It is not a CEL evaluator; no API server ran these expressions.
